# Erdős Problem #246 — Benchmark

## Problem-level classification

The problem is classified as PROVED (LEAN) on erdosproblems.com. The benchmark selection was verified at the problem level, not via a solved variant or sub-result.

## Canonical statement

> Let $(a,b)=1$. The set $\{a^kb^l: k,l\geq 0\}$ is complete — that is, every large integer is the sum of distinct integers of the form $a^kb^l$ with $k,l\geq 0$.

This statement is the benchmark target. No paraphrase or strengthening is to replace it during the blind phase.

## Blind-mode restrictions

Until proof-freeze or explicit failure:

1. Do not inspect the historical proof by Birch.
2. Do not inspect later proofs or generalisations.
3. Do not inspect the formal proof.
4. External verification is limited to problem-level status and canonical statement.

## Objective

Construct an independent proof, audit it, freeze it if valid, and only then compare it with the reference literature.

## Selection rationale

This is the fourth benchmark. It differs from #1125 (global functional inequality), #275 (covering systems and finite-dimensional algebra), and #303 (Ramsey/reciprocal transfer). The statement is short and algebraic, while the central question is constructive/additive-combinatorial: completeness of the multiplicative semigroup generated by two coprime integers.
